# Historical Model

## Historical standard

The project aims to be strict where reliable historical sources permit.

The simulation must not pretend that uncertain details are known facts.

## Evidence categories

Historical scenario data is classified as:

### Attested

Directly supported by reliable evidence.

### Plausibly reconstructed

Not directly attested, but inferred from strong historical context and comparable evidence.

### Systemically completed

Required for the simulation but not recoverable in sufficient detail from historical evidence.

## Provenance

Historical scenario records should be able to carry provenance such as:

- certainty category
- source references
- reconstruction notes

## Historical people

Real historical people may appear when evidence provides a sufficiently reliable basis.

Known initial facts may be encoded.

After scenario start, they are normal simulation actors and are not protected from systemic consequences.

## Historical outcomes

A known historical outcome should not automatically be forced if the local simulation can plausibly diverge.

Example principle:

A historically documented external war may enter the scenario as an external condition.

The exact fate of a local merchant inside the simulated region should normally emerge from the simulation.

## External historical events

Events originating outside the fully simulated area may be injected as external causes when necessary.

Examples:

- distant war
- ruler succession
- major external trade restriction
- external epidemic pressure

Local consequences should still propagate through ordinary systems.

## Local law and institutions

Legal, commercial, religious, political, and guild rules are historically local.

Avoid treating medieval Europe as if it had one universal legal or economic system.

## Units and currencies

Historical:

- coins
- weights
- measures
- denominations

may vary by place and time.

Internally they may map to canonical deterministic units, while actors and UI use historically appropriate representations.

## Historical change

There should be no artificial era switch at a century boundary.

Long-term change emerges from:

- law
- institutions
- knowledge
- technology
- politics
- demographic change
- trade
- social practice

A long-running save may therefore diverge substantially from recorded history.
